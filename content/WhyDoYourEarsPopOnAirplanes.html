06 — Scientific Notes & Sources

What actually happens?

The middle ear is an air-filled space behind the eardrum. The Eustachian tube connects it to the back of the nose and upper throat.

When airplane cabin pressure changes during ascent or descent, pressure on the two sides of the eardrum can become unequal. This can make your ears feel full or your hearing temporarily muffled.

Swallowing, yawning, or chewing can briefly open the Eustachian tube, allowing air to move and helping equalize the pressure. That familiar pop is often the sensation or sound of pressure equalizing.

Pressure equalization can be harder during descent. Colds and allergies can also make it more difficult if the tube becomes swollen or blocked.

Practical note: Swallowing, yawning, or chewing gum may help. If trying to equalize pressure by holding your nose and blowing, do it gently, never forcefully. Severe pain or symptoms that persist after flying deserve medical advice.
