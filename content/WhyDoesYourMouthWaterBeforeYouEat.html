06 — Scientific Notes & Sources

What actually happens?

Your salivary glands produce saliva throughout the day, but the amount can increase when you see, smell, taste, or even think about food.

This is part of a group of anticipatory reactions called the cephalic phase response. The word "cephalic" refers to the head.

Your brain processes food-related signals and activates pathways in the autonomic nervous system. These signals stimulate your salivary glands to release more saliva.

Why does your body do this? Saliva moistens food, helps you chew and swallow, and contains enzymes such as amylase that begin breaking down starch.

In short: Your body doesn't wait until food reaches your stomach. It starts preparing for digestion before the first bite.
