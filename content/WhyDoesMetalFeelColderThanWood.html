Scientific notes & sources

1. Are metal and wood actually the same temperature? Yes. If both objects have been sitting in the same room long enough, they will usually reach thermal equilibrium with their surroundings. A metal spoon and a wooden spoon might both be around 24°C.

2. Why does metal feel colder? Your skin is usually warmer than room-temperature objects. When you touch them, thermal energy moves from your hand into the objects. Metal conducts thermal energy much faster than wood, so your skin loses heat more quickly.

3. What is thermal conductivity? Thermal conductivity describes how effectively a material transfers heat. Metals generally have high thermal conductivity, while wood is a relatively poor conductor and acts as an insulator.

4. Does this work in reverse? Absolutely. If metal and wood are both hotter than your skin, the metal can transfer heat into your hand faster, making it feel hotter than the wood.

5. The important distinction Your sense of touch does not simply measure an object's temperature. It also responds to how quickly thermal energy moves between your skin and the object.

Accuracy note: Avoid saying that metal is always colder than wood. The comparison assumes both objects are initially at the same temperature and that temperature is below your skin temperature.
