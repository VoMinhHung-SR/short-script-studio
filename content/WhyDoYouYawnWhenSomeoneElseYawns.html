Scientific Accuracy Notes

Yawning can be triggered by seeing, hearing, reading about, or even thinking about another person's yawn. Researchers call this contagious yawning. The exact mechanism is still uncertain. Social closeness and attention may influence the response, but it should not be presented as a direct test of someone's empathy.
  • Cleveland Clinic — Yawning: Definition & Causes
    Explains common triggers, including seeing, hearing and thinking about yawning.
  • Massen & Gallup (2017) — Why Contagious Yawning Does Not (Yet) Equate to Empathy
    A critical review showing that evidence linking contagious yawning and empathy is inconsistent.
  • Auditory Contagious Yawning Is Highest Between Friends and Family Members
    Research exploring the influence of social relationships on contagious yawning.
  • Yearning to Yawn: The Neural Basis of Contagious Yawning
    Brain-imaging research examining neural activity when people watch others yawn.

Accuracy note: The relationship between contagious yawning and empathy remains debated. Some studies find associations with social closeness or empathy-related measures, while reviews find the overall evidence inconclusive. Avoid saying that yawning proves someone is empathetic.
