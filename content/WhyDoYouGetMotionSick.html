06 — Scientific Notes & Sources

What actually happens?

Your brain combines information from your eyes, inner ear, muscles, and joints to understand how your body is moving.

When you read a book or look at your phone in a moving car, your eyes focus on something that appears stationary. But your inner ear and body still detect the vehicle's movement.

This mismatch between sensory signals is the most widely accepted explanation for motion sickness. It can trigger symptoms such as nausea, dizziness, sweating, and discomfort.

Looking outside toward the horizon can help because your eyes get a clearer visual reference for the movement. Avoiding reading and getting fresh air may also help.

Important: Sensory conflict is the leading explanation, but motion sickness is complex and individual sensitivity varies. Not everyone experiences it in the same way.
