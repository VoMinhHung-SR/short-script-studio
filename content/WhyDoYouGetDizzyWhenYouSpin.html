06 — Scientific Notes & Sources

What actually happens?

The inner ear contains three semicircular canals filled with fluid. These canals help detect rotational movement of the head.

When you spin, the canals move with your head, but the fluid inside briefly lags behind because of inertia. This movement bends tiny sensory structures and sends signals to your brain.

When you suddenly stop, the fluid can keep moving briefly. The sensory signals may then suggest that you're still rotating, even though your body has stopped. This mismatch contributes to the dizzy, spinning sensation.

Important: This explains the familiar temporary dizziness after spinning. Not all dizziness has this cause; persistent or unexplained dizziness can have other causes.
