06 — Scientific Notes & Sources

What actually happens?

Chili peppers contain capsaicin, the chemical responsible for their burning sensation. Capsaicin activates sensory receptors, including TRPV1, which are involved in detecting heat and irritation.

In some people, spicy food triggers a nerve reflex that stimulates glands in the nasal lining to produce watery secretions. This can make the nose run while eating.

This reaction is called gustatory rhinitis. It is generally a non-allergic response, rather than the immune reaction involved in a typical food allergy.

The response usually settles after the trigger food is gone. Not everyone experiences it, and other foods or even very hot foods can trigger similar symptoms in some people.

Important: A runny nose alone after spicy food does not automatically mean an allergy. But symptoms such as hives, swelling of the lips or throat, or difficulty breathing need urgent medical attention.
