Scientific notes & sources

1. Why is your breath normally invisible? The air you exhale contains water vapor, which is water in its gaseous state. Water vapor itself is invisible, so you normally cannot see the moisture leaving your lungs.

2. What happens when your breath meets cold air? Your exhaled breath is warm and contains a lot of moisture. When it mixes with sufficiently cold surrounding air, it cools toward its dew point. Some water vapor then condenses into countless tiny liquid water droplets.

3. Why does it look like smoke? Those tiny suspended water droplets scatter light, making the cloud visible. What looks like smoke is actually a miniature cloud of liquid water droplets.

4. Why does the cloud disappear? As the droplets mix with surrounding air, they can evaporate back into invisible water vapor. That's why your breath cloud usually appears briefly before fading away.

5. Does temperature explain everything? Not entirely. Humidity also matters. The visibility of your breath depends on how much moisture is in the air and whether the mixture cools enough to reach saturation and condense.

Accuracy note: The white cloud is not water vapor itself. Water vapor is invisible. The visible cloud consists mainly of tiny liquid water droplets, although ice crystals may also form under sufficiently cold conditions.
