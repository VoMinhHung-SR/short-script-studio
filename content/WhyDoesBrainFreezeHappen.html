Scientific notes & sources

1. What is brain freeze? Brain freeze is a short-lasting headache triggered by cold food or drinks touching the roof of your mouth or the back of your throat. Its medical name is cold-stimulus headache.

2. Why does your forehead hurt? The trigeminal nerve carries sensory information from the face and parts of the head. Cold stimulation in the mouth can activate this pain pathway, but the brain may perceive the pain in the forehead or temples instead. This is an example of referred pain.

3. What happens to your blood vessels? One leading explanation suggests that sudden cooling causes blood vessels in the palate to constrict and then rapidly widen. Changes around these vessels may contribute to activating pain signals. The exact mechanism is still being investigated.

4. How can you stop it? Stop eating the cold food and warm the roof of your mouth by pressing your tongue against it. Drinking a room-temperature beverage may also help. Eating or drinking more slowly can reduce the chance of triggering another episode.

Important: Never claim that the brain literally freezes. The pain is caused by a cold stimulus in the mouth or throat, not by ice forming inside the brain. The precise biological mechanism is not completely settled.
