Scientific notes & sources

1. What is static electricity? Static electricity happens when positive and negative electrical charges become unbalanced. An object can hold an excess of electrons or have fewer electrons than usual.

2. How does your body become charged? When certain materials come into contact and separate, electrons can transfer between their surfaces. Walking across a carpet, for example, can leave your body with an electrical charge.

3. Why does touching metal cause a shock? Metal is a good electrical conductor. When your charged body approaches a metal object with a different electrical potential, the electric field can become strong enough to break down the air between them. A tiny spark transfers charge very quickly.

4. Why are static shocks more common in dry weather? Moisture on surfaces can help electrical charges dissipate. In dry conditions, charges tend to remain on insulating materials for longer, making noticeable static discharges more likely.

5. Why does your hair sometimes stand up? When hair strands acquire similar electrical charges, they repel one another. This pushes the strands apart and makes them stand up.

Accuracy note: Not every static shock happens through exactly the same charging process. Contact and separation between materials can transfer charge in different ways. Avoid claiming that friction literally creates electrons. It generally redistributes existing electrical charges.
