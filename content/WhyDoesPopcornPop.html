Scientific notes & sources

1. What's inside a popcorn kernel? A popcorn kernel contains water, starch, and a hard outer covering called the pericarp. The starch-rich interior is known as the endosperm. Popcorn typically contains around 13–14% moisture, although the ideal amount varies with the kernel.

2. Why does pressure build up? When heated, the water inside the kernel turns into vapor. The strong pericarp helps trap that vapor, allowing pressure to build while the starch softens and gelatinizes.

3. What actually causes the explosion? Once the internal pressure becomes too great, the pericarp ruptures. The pressurized water vapor rapidly expands, pushing the softened starch outward. The starch expands and cools into the familiar fluffy popcorn structure.

4. Why does popcorn jump? Research suggests that escaping starch forms a small leg-like structure that pushes against the cooking surface, launching the kernel into the air.

5. Why does it make that popping sound? A scientific study found that the release of water vapor is consistent with the origin of the characteristic popping sound. The exact dynamics of the sound and movement are more complicated than a simple explosion.

Accuracy note: Popcorn does not simply explode because water boils. Its special outer shell, moisture content, pressure buildup, and softening starch all work together. The temperature and pressure values vary with the kernel and experimental conditions.
