Scientific Accuracy Notes

The feeling that life speeds up with age is a subjective judgment, especially when looking back over months or years. Researchers have proposed several explanations, including routine, memory formation, attention and the way we compare different periods of our lives. There is no single proven explanation that accounts for everyone.
  • Frontiers in Psychology (2026) — The Time of Your Life: Mapping the Mechanisms Behind Life Tempo Judgments
    A recent review covering routine, memory, attention, motivation and emotional meaning.
  • Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin (2024) — Why Life Moves Fast
    Research examining routine, autobiographical memory and the subjective passage of time.
  • Memory & Cognition (2026) — Memory Encoding and Age-Related Differences in Subjective Time
    A study of 120 adults aged 20–91 examining memory formation and perceived passage of time.
  • Age, Time, and Decision Making — A Review
    Discusses different theories about subjective time perception and aging.

Accuracy note: Avoid claiming that the brain literally speeds up or that one year mathematically becomes shorter as you age. The ratio explanation is a popular hypothesis, not a complete scientific answer. Also distinguish how fast time feels while experiencing an event from how quickly a period seems to have passed when remembering it later.
